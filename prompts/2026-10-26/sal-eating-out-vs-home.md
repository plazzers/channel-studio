1. Hook: everybody knows cooking at home is cheaper. What surprises people is how much, and why. Promise: where a restaurant bill really goes, 10 restaurant favorites with a verdict on each — make it at home or worth ordering out — and a free calculator so viewers can run their own numbers.
2. Where the bill goes: ingredients are usually only a modest slice of the menu price. You also pay for the cooks, the servers, the dishwasher, the rent, the lights and the insurance. Many restaurants run on thin profits, so this isn't a rip-off; it's the cost of a night off. Then the extras people forget: drinks, appetizers, dessert, tax, the tip, and delivery fees.
3. Favorite 1: fettuccine Alfredo — pasta, butter, cheese. Verdict: make it at home, about 20 minutes.
4. Favorite 2: spaghetti and meatballs — cheap, freezes well, better the next day. Verdict: home, make a double batch.
5. Favorite 3: chicken parm — a little work, but one of the biggest gaps between menu price and what it takes at home. Verdict: home.
6. Favorite 4: shrimp scampi — fast, simple; the shrimp is the main cost. Verdict: home.
7. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description — for the nights you do go out, so the money goes further.
8. Favorite 5: soup and breadsticks — soup is cheap to make and freezes well. Verdict: home.
9. Favorite 6: house salad and the breadbasket — usually some of the cheapest food on the table to make. Verdict: home, and save restaurant money for the main.
10. Favorite 7: the smash burger and fries — a hot pan does most of the work; fries without a fryer are the hard part. Verdict: close call; burgers at home, fries out.
11. Favorite 8: the steak — a good cut costs real money at home too, and a hot pan and a thermometer get you close. Verdict: close call; home for a weeknight, out for a celebration.
12. Favorite 9: tiramisu and restaurant desserts — few ingredients, makes a whole pan. Verdict: home.
13. Favorite 10: drinks — soda, wine and cocktails often carry the biggest markup on the bill. Verdict: this is where the quickest savings are.
14. When eating out is worth every penny: dishes that need equipment you don't have (a wood-fired oven, a deep fryer), long or tricky dishes you'd never make, the people and the night off itself. Sal is not anti-restaurant; he wants the money spent where it counts.
15. Payoff: make the simple favorites at home, go out for what you can't make, and skip the extras that inflate the bill. Point viewers to the free Restaurant or Home? calculator in the description to try their own dishes with their own local prices.
16. Outro: Sal's Restaurant Copycat Cookbook for making restaurant favorites at home, and Sal's Kitchen App; tease next video.
