1. Hook: most people never look at their water heater until there's water on the floor. Tank water heaters usually give warnings first, and most of them can be spotted in five minutes with a flashlight. Promise: the 7 signs, what each one usually means, and when it's time to call a plumber. This video is about standard tank water heaters; tankless units are different.
2. Sign 1 (check this one tonight): its age. Read the label on the side of the tank. Some show a manufacture date; many hide it in the serial number, and the pattern differs by brand, so look up the maker's serial number guide or ask a plumber instead of guessing. Say that a standard tank commonly lasts somewhere around 8 to 12 years, that it varies a lot, and that age alone doesn't mean failure. Tip: take a phone photo of the label.
3. Sign 2: rust or corrosion. Rust at the base or seams of the tank is usually the beginning of the end. Corrosion or crusty buildup on the pipe connections on top is worth noting and watching.
4. Sign 3: water on the floor or in the drain pan. It can come from a fitting, the relief valve or the tank itself. Dry the area, then check again later. If it's the tank, it won't fix itself.
5. Sign 4: rusty or discolored hot water. If only the hot water looks rusty, the tank may be corroding inside; if hot and cold both look off, the cause may be the pipes or the water supply. Either way, worth a plumber's look.
6. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description — the water heater is one of the 25 things on it.
7. Sign 5: popping, rumbling or banging. Often sediment building up at the bottom of the tank. Many makers recommend draining some water from the tank on a schedule; follow the manual for your model, and if the drain valve looks old or stuck, leave it to a plumber.
8. Sign 6: the relief valve and its pipe. Point out the temperature and pressure relief valve near the top and the pipe running down from it. Look, don't fiddle: a dripping valve or a wet pipe end is a sign to call a plumber. The pipe should never be capped, plugged or removed. If the label says to test the valve and you're unsure, have a plumber do it; the water that comes out is scalding hot.
9. Sign 7: less hot water than it used to make, or temperature that swings. It can be sediment, a failing part or a tank that's simply getting old. For a gas unit: soot or scorch marks near the burner area, or a flame that doesn't look right through the window, means stop and call a licensed pro.
10. Bonus, cheap peace of mind: a drain pan under the tank where possible, a battery water-leak alarm on the floor next to it, a working CO alarm nearby for gas units, nothing stored against the tank, and know where the cold-water shutoff on top of the tank is.
11. Payoff: the 7 signs in one line each: age, rust, water on the floor, rusty hot water, rumbling, relief valve, less hot water. One sign means watch it. Two or three on an older tank usually mean start planning a replacement, on your schedule instead of in an emergency.

SAFETY/ACCURACY: Educational only. Keep hands off hot pipes, the burner area and the top of the tank; hot water can scald. Gas smell: leave the house, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside. A leaking tank: shut off the cold-water supply and the power or gas the way the manual describes, then call a plumber.
