STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments, mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: the steak arrives sizzling, dark crust, glossy butter, pink from edge to edge. Then you try it at home and get gray and chewy. The steakhouse doesn't have a secret seasoning. It has an order of operations. Promise: the 7 steps of the steakhouse method, all in a regular home pan, plus the mistakes that ruin an expensive steak.
2. Step 1: pick the right steak. Ribeye, strip or sirloin, cut thick enough (about an inch or a little more) so it can brown before the middle overcooks. Thin steaks overcook. Ask the butcher counter; they'll cut it for you.
3. Step 2: salt early. Ideally the night before, uncovered on a rack in the fridge; at least about 45 minutes ahead if not. Explain simply: salt draws moisture out, then it goes back in, and the surface dries. The worst time to salt is a few minutes before cooking.
4. Step 3: dry it. Paper towels, right before the pan. A wet steak steams; a dry steak sears. Sal's line: "Water is the enemy of crust."
5. Step 4: the pan. Heavy cast iron or thick stainless, heated longer than feels reasonable, a thin film of high-heat oil, wait for the first wisp of smoke. Turn on the fan and open a window.
6. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description — including what to ask before you order the steak at a restaurant.
7. Step 5: leave it alone, then flip. Let the first side release on its own with a real crust. After that, flipping often is fine and cooks it evenly. Hold thick steaks on their fat edge for a moment.
8. Step 6: butter goes in last. Lower the heat a little, add butter, smashed garlic and thyme or rosemary, tilt the pan and spoon the foaming butter over the steak. Smashed cloves, not minced: minced garlic burns.
9. Step 7: thermometer and rest. Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part. Say clearly: the USDA safe minimum for beef steaks is 145°F followed by a 3-minute rest; many people order steak less done at restaurants as a personal choice, but 145°F with the rest is the official number, especially for kids, older guests or anyone who wants to play it safe. Pull it a little early because it keeps rising while it rests. Then slice against the grain.
10. The 5 mistakes, fast: a wet steak, salting right before cooking, a pan that isn't hot enough, crowding the pan, cutting it right away.
11. The steakhouse plate at home: mashed potatoes, something green, and extra garlic butter — Sal's Garlic Butter Sauce is a free recipe on his guides site (point to it, don't read the amounts). Mention the free Restaurant or Home? calculator to compare a steakhouse night for two with cooking it at home.
12. Payoff: the 7 steps in one line each — pick it thick, salt early, dry it, hot heavy pan, leave it then flip, butter last, thermometer and rest. "Salt early, pan hot, butter last."
13. Outro: the full Pan-Seared Steak with Garlic Butter recipe is in Sal's Restaurant Copycat Cookbook, with the sides and the lava cake dessert for a full steakhouse night; tease the next video (25 forgotten red-sauce restaurant dishes).
