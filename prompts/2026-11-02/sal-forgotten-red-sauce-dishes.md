1. Hook: the red-sauce joint. Checkered tablecloths, a candle in a straw-wrapped bottle, a basket of bread, a plate big enough for two. Menus have changed and many of these dishes are harder to find now. Promise: 25 classics from those menus, what each one was, why people loved it, and one tip to make it at home.
2. Part 1 — The starters (5): clams casino; fried calamari with marinara; mozzarella in carrozza (fried mozzarella sandwich); stuffed mushrooms; the big antipasto platter with cured meats, cheese, olives and peppers.
3. Part 2 — The veal and chicken cutlets (6): veal saltimbocca; veal or chicken piccata (lemon, butter, capers); chicken Francese (egg-dipped, lemon-butter sauce); chicken or veal Marsala; chicken scarpariello (chicken on the bone with sausage and peppers in a vinegar pan sauce); chicken Sorrentino (eggplant, prosciutto and mozzarella on top).
4. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description.
5. Part 3 — The baked pasta trays (5): baked ziti; manicotti; stuffed shells; eggplant rollatini; lasagna the old way with ricotta and meat sauce.
6. Part 4 — From the sea (4): linguine with white clam sauce; shrimp or lobster fra diavolo; zuppa di pesce (the mixed seafood stew); shrimp scampi the red-sauce-joint way.
7. Part 5 — The sweet finish (5): cannoli; spumoni; biscuit tortoni; rainbow cookies (the tri-color almond layer cookies); zabaglione whisked warm.
8. For each dish: one or two sentences on what it is, why the old restaurants loved it (cheap cuts made special, one sauce used five ways, made ahead in big trays), and Sal's one home tip (pound cutlets thin and even; dredge right before frying; let baked pasta rest before cutting; never boil the clams hard; fill cannoli shells just before serving so they stay crisp). Keep each dish short so the pace stays quick.
9. A short honest note: some of these are Italian-American restaurant creations, not dishes you'd find in Italy, and that's part of their charm. Don't give specific invention stories, dates or inventor names.
10. Payoff: the 25 dishes are proof that one good red sauce, a few cutlets and a hot oven can feed a whole table. Pick three for a Sunday dinner or a holiday buffet. Point to Sal's free Sunday Marinara recipe on his guides site as the base for half of them.
11. Outro: Sal's Italian Kitchen for the Italian dishes, and the Copycat Cookbook for the restaurant favorites like chicken parm and shrimp scampi; tease the next video.
