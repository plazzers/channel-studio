1. Hook: water damage rarely starts with a flood. It starts with a slow drip, a clogged downspout or a hose nobody looked at in years. Most of it can be caught with a flashlight and ten minutes, and none of these checks cost anything. Promise: 9 free checks, from the roof edge down to the basement floor, and what to do if you find something.
2. Check 1: know your main water shutoff. Find it, make sure you can reach it, and hang a bright tag on it. Turn it gently; if it's stiff or seems stuck, don't force it, ask a plumber. Everyone in the house should know where it is.
3. Check 2: the gutters and downspouts. After the leaves fall, look for overflowing gutters, sagging sections and downspouts that dump water right at the foundation. Water should land several feet away from the house. Stay on the ground or a safe step; anything high is for a pro or someone with the right ladder and help.
4. Check 3: the ground around the house. The soil should slope away from the foundation, not toward it. Look for low spots, mulch or soil piled up against the siding, and puddles that sit by the walls after rain.
5. Check 4: under every sink. Flashlight, look at the pipes and the cabinet floor. Water stains, warped wood or a musty smell mean a slow leak. Sinks on outside walls also matter for freezing in winter.
6. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description — it walks through 25 checks like these in one weekend.
7. Check 5: the washing machine hoses. Pull the machine out a little if you can and look at the hoses: bulges, cracks or rust at the ends are warning signs. Many makers suggest replacing rubber hoses every few years; follow the manual. Know where the two valves behind the machine are.
8. Check 6: the water heater area. Look for water on the floor or in the drain pan, rust at the base and drips at the pipes on top. Point back to the water heater video for the full list. Keep hands off hot pipes.
9. Check 7: the silent toilet leak. A few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait without flushing, and if color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking. Also look at the floor around the base for soft spots or stains.
10. Check 8: the ceilings and walls. Walk the house and look up: ceilings below bathrooms, near outside walls and under the attic. New stains, bubbling paint or sagging drywall mean water is coming from somewhere. Take a photo and date it so you can see if it grows.
11. Check 9: the basement or crawlspace after a heavy rain. Damp corners, white crusty deposits on the walls, a musty smell, rust on metal at the floor. Point to the free guide on signs of water in a basement.
12. Winter bonus, before the first hard freeze: disconnect garden hoses, know which pipes run along outside walls or through the garage, and keep the heat on when you travel. The free Before the First Freeze checklist is in the description.
13. Payoff: the 9 checks in one line each — shutoff, gutters, grading, sinks, washer hoses, water heater, toilet, ceilings, basement. What to do when you find water: dry it, find the source, and shut off the water to that fixture or the house if it's active. Small, slow leaks are worth a plumber's look sooner rather than later.
14. Outro: Walter's Home Check Manual has a room-by-room version of these checks; tease the next video (the 9 things around your electrical panel that mean call an electrician).
SAFETY/ACCURACY: Educational only. Never go on the roof; gutters are checked from the ground or a safe step, with a helper. Water and electricity: if water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of the water and call an electrician or the utility. Shutoff valves: turn gently, never force; stuck valves are a plumber job. Mold beyond a small area or any health concerns: follow EPA guidance and talk to a professional.
