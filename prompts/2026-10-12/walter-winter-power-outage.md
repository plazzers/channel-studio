1. Hook: when the power goes out in January, most furnaces stop too, even gas ones, because the blower and controls need electricity. The house starts cooling right away. Promise: 9 things to do before the storm, so an outage is an inconvenience, not a disaster.
2. Thing 1: Find and tag the main water shutoff, and make sure it turns. If a frozen pipe bursts, this valve saves the floors.
3. Thing 2: Test smoke and CO alarms, and make sure at least some CO alarms have battery power or battery backup. Plug-in alarms without backup stop when the power does.
4. Thing 3: Build a small outage kit, as Ready.gov suggests: flashlights and spare batteries (not candles), a battery or hand-crank radio, phone power bank, water, food that needs no cooking, blankets. Phone numbers written on paper.
5. Thing 4: Learn the manual release on the garage door opener, and try it once in daylight.
6. Thing 5: Plan the pipes: which pipes are on outside walls, in the crawlspace or garage; open cabinet doors under sinks and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. Never thaw a pipe with an open flame or torch; if one bursts, shut off the main and call a plumber.
7. Mid-video: the free Before the First Freeze checklist is the first link in the description.
8. Thing 6: Pick your one warm room now: lowest floor, fewest windows, doors you can close. Curtains closed at night, layers, hats and socks.
9. Thing 7: Decide now what you will NOT use for heat: never the gas oven or stovetop, a grill, a camp stove, a patio heater, or a generator indoors or in the garage. All can fill a house with carbon monoxide (NFPA and CPSC warn about this every winter). Space heaters only if made for indoor use, three feet clear, plugged into the wall.
10. Thing 8: If you own a portable generator: read the manual now, pick its spot outside, far from the house with the exhaust pointed away (CPSC recommends at least 20 feet), heavy-duty outdoor cords, never plugged into a wall outlet; connecting to house wiring needs a transfer switch installed by a licensed electrician. Battery CO alarms inside while it runs.
11. Thing 9: Fridge and freezer plan: keep the doors closed (Ready.gov: about 4 hours for a closed fridge, about 48 hours for a full freezer). When in doubt, throw it out. And know when to leave: if the house gets too cold, go to family or a warming center, and check on older neighbors who live alone.
12. Payoff: the one-afternoon list — tag the shutoff, test alarms, build the kit, choose the warm room, read the generator manual. And after the power returns: walk the house and look for leaks from pipes that froze.
13. Outro: The Home Check Manual for the full seasonal and room-by-room checks; mention Walter's free guide "Winter Power Outage Checklist" on the guides site in the description; tease next video on the house features that make a home hard to sell.
