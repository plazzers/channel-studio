NARRATOR: Walter, a calm, experienced guide to checking a house before you buy, speaking in first person to home buyers. Plain language, warm, practical. Never claims licenses, certifications or a specific career history.

STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments, mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: most buyers think about the next 5 years in a house, not the day they sell it. But some features can shrink the number of future buyers. Promise: 9 features to think twice about, and the question to ask for each. Not "never buy", but "buy with your eyes open and a fair price".
2. Feature 1: Work done without permits (finished basement, garage turned into a bedroom, addition). Ask for permits and final sign-offs; your agent can help look up records. It can cause trouble with insurance, appraisal or the next sale.
3. Feature 2: Odd floor plans, like a bedroom you can only reach through another bedroom, or one bathroom for a large house. Many future buyers will notice.
4. Feature 3: Water problems: signs of a wet basement, a lot that slopes toward the house, or a flood zone. Ask about flood insurance and drainage; have the inspector look closely.
5. Feature 4: Location issues you can't fix: a very busy road, a commercial building or rail line right behind the yard. Visit at rush hour and at night.
6. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description (good for the first walk-through of any house you own or buy).
7. Feature 5: Big systems all near the end of their life at once: roof, furnace, AC, water heater. Ask their ages and get records; plan for it in your offer.
8. Feature 6: Very personal or over-the-top renovations: unusual colors and finishes, a pool or feature that some buyers see as upkeep. Fine if you love it, but know the next buyer may not. Say "some buyers", never "all".
9. Feature 7: Too-perfect flips: fresh paint everywhere, cheap finishes, and no paperwork for the work. Point back to the bad-flip red flags.
10. Feature 8: Hard-to-insure items in some areas: very old wiring types, older panels, an old oil tank. Ask your insurance agent before you buy, not after.
11. Feature 9: Steep driveways, lots of stairs and no bedroom on the main floor: a real issue for many older buyers. Think about who might buy it next.
12. Payoff: the question for every feature — "Can I fix this, and will the next buyer care?" Things you can fix are negotiating points; things you can't change belong in the price.
13. Outro: Walter's Red Flag Checklist for the full list to take to showings; tease next video.
