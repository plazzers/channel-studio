NARRATOR: Chef Sal Romano, a high-energy, confident Italian-American TV chef speaking to camera in first person.

STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments, mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: a whole aisle of pasta, and most bags look the same. But two bags at nearly the same price can cook very differently. Promise: 7 things to look for on the bag and in the box, so your sauce finally sticks. No brands named, just what to read and what to feel.
2. Sign 1: "Bronze-cut" or "bronze die" on the label. Pasta pushed through bronze dies has a rough, dull, slightly chalky surface that grabs sauce. Smooth, shiny pasta (usually from non-bronze dies) lets sauce slide off.
3. Sign 2: The ingredient list. Durum wheat semolina (and water) should be the main thing. In Italy, dried pasta is made from durum wheat semolina by law. Explain why durum holds its shape.
4. Sign 3: Look and feel. Pale, creamy-wheat color and a matte surface vs very bright yellow and glassy. Run a finger over it (in the box window or at home).
5. Sign 4: Slow drying. Some labels mention slow drying at lower temperatures; many cooks find it keeps more flavor and a better bite. Keep it as "many cooks find", not a scientific claim.
6. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description.
7. Sign 5: The pasta water test at home. Good pasta often leaves cloudy, starchy water — which is exactly what you want to save for the sauce.
8. Sign 6: Protected names worth knowing, like "Pasta di Gragnano IGP", a protected geographical indication for pasta from Gragnano near Naples. Nice to know, not required for a great dinner.
9. Sign 7: Price isn't everything. Many affordable and store-brand pastas are bronze-cut; read the bag instead of the price tag. Buy one good bag and one cheap bag and cook them side by side once.
10. Bonus: match the shape to the sauce — ridges and tubes for chunky sauces, long strands for smooth and oily sauces, small shapes for soup.
11. Payoff: the 10-second label check — bronze-cut, durum wheat semolina, matte and pale. Then cook it right: big pot, salted water, save a mug of pasta water, finish in the pan.
12. Outro: Sal's Italian Kitchen for the sauces that deserve good pasta; tease next video (25 forgotten Italian-American dinners).
