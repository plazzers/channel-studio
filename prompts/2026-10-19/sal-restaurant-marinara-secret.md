1. Hook: you buy good tomatoes, you simmer for an hour, and it still tastes flat next to the restaurant's. The secret isn't one ingredient. It's a handful of habits. Promise: the 7 habits behind restaurant marinara, and Sal's free Sunday Marinara recipe on his guides site.
2. Secret 1: the tomatoes. Good canned whole tomatoes, crushed by hand, often taste better than pre-crushed. Taste them straight from the can; they change from can to can.
3. Secret 2: the oil and garlic at low heat. Gentle heat until the garlic is pale gold, never brown. Burnt garlic makes the whole pot bitter.
4. Secret 3: salt early and taste at every step, not just at the end.
5. Secret 4: the simmer. Long enough to cook off the raw, tinny taste and thicken, not so long it turns dull. Stir and scrape the bottom.
6. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description.
7. Secret 5: balancing acidity. Sour sauce? Simmer longer first, then a small pinch of sugar or a small piece of butter, tasting after each change. Sal's line: "it's a fix, not a recipe."
8. Secret 6: the Parmesan rind and fresh basil at the end. The rind simmers in for savory depth and comes out before serving; basil goes in at the very end so it stays fresh.
9. Secret 7: the finish in the pan. Restaurants toss the pasta in the sauce with a splash of starchy pasta water for a minute, so the sauce clings. Sauce on top of naked pasta is the home-cook giveaway.
10. Bonus: make a big batch and freeze it in portions — the restaurant prep habit that gives you a weeknight dinner in 15 minutes.
11. Payoff: the 7 habits in one line each. Say that the full Sunday Marinara recipe is free on Sal's guides site (sal/guides/sunday-marinara-sauce) — it's one of the free samples from the Copycat Cookbook.
12. Outro: the Copycat Cookbook and Sal's Italian Kitchen for the dishes that go on top of the sauce; tease next video.
