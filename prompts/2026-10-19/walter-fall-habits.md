NARRATOR: Walter, a calm, experienced guide to looking after a house, speaking in first person to homeowners. Plain language, warm, practical. Never claims licenses, certifications or a specific career history.

1. Hook: some neighbors never seem to have a winter emergency. It's rarely luck. It's a short list of habits they repeat every fall, usually on one calm Saturday. Promise: the 7 habits, in the order to do them, and what to look for at each step. This is about habits, not a long pre-winter inspection list.
2. Habit 1: They walk the outside of the house after the leaves come down. Gutters, downspouts pointing away from the house, soil sloping away from the foundation, gaps around pipes and wires. Everything from the ground or a safe step; roofs and tall ladders are a job for a pro.
3. Habit 2: They shut off and drain the outside faucets and put the garden hoses away. Find the indoor shutoff valve for each outdoor faucet if the house has one; frost-free faucets still need the hose disconnected.
4. Habit 3: They start the heat early, on a mild day, not on the first freezing night. Listen and smell: a little dust smell the first time is common; anything that keeps smelling, a gas smell, or a furnace that won't stay on means call a licensed HVAC technician. They also book the tune-up before the busy season.
5. Habit 4: They test every smoke and CO alarm and replace the batteries if needed, and check the date on the back of each alarm. Many makers say replace smoke alarms about every 10 years; CO alarms often sooner (check the label).
6. Mid-video: the free Before the First Freeze checklist is the first link in the description.
7. Habit 5 (the one most people skip): they find and test the main water shutoff, and tag it. If a pipe bursts in January, this is the valve that matters. Turn it gently; if it's stuck, have a plumber look at it now, not during a flood.
8. Habit 6: They look at the fireplace and chimney before the first fire: damper opens and closes, no daylight cracks in the firebox, a cap on top (from the ground). Many safety groups recommend a yearly chimney inspection by a certified chimney sweep.
9. Habit 7: They get ready for the first outage: flashlights and batteries where they can be found in the dark, phone power bank, a plan for the sump pump, and they know generators only ever run outside, far from windows and doors.
10. Bonus: they write it down. A date on the furnace filter, a note in a notebook or an app, so next fall they know what was done.
11. Payoff: it's one Saturday, in this order: outside walk, water, heat, alarms, shutoff, fireplace, outage kit. Most of it is free and most of it is looking.
12. Outro: The Home Check Manual has the full fall section room by room; tease the next video.
