TITLE: What I'd Never Order at an Italian Chain Restaurant (And What to Get Instead)
NARRATOR: Chef Sal Romano, a high-energy, confident Italian-American TV chef speaking to camera in first person.
AUDIENCE: US diners and home cooks, 30-70, who eat at big casual Italian restaurants with the family and want better value.
LENGTH: 14 minutes (about 2400 spoken words)
OUTPUT FORMAT: Plain spoken script only, ready for voice generation. No stage directions.
STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments, mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: the big Italian chain menu is long, the photos are pretty, and some dishes are great value while others are mostly markup. Promise: 7 things Sal personally skips, why, and what he orders instead. This is Sal's taste and value opinion, not a review of any one restaurant.
2. Skip 1: the most expensive "combo" or sampler platter of three pastas. Often it's three dishes you could get separately, smaller portions of each. Instead: one dish you really want, done well.
3. Skip 2: plain spaghetti with marinara at a big price. It's the cheapest thing to make at home. Instead: something with a technique you don't do at home, like a braise or a dish finished in the pan.
4. Skip 3: the soda and the refill-heavy drinks if you're watching the bill, and the "specialty" sangria pitcher without asking the price. Drinks are where a lot of the margin is. Instead: water, and order one drink you enjoy.
5. Skip 4: the "today's special" without asking the price. Specials can be great, but ask first. Instead: ask "what's the price?" and "what's fresh today?" — it's polite and normal.
6. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description.
7. Skip 5: Alfredo drowned in heavy sauce. Real fettuccine Alfredo is mostly butter, Parmigiano and pasta water; many restaurant versions use a lot of cream. Instead: if you love it, make it at home the simple way (tease the Copycat Cookbook).
8. Skip 6: dessert from the dessert tray that looks identical everywhere. To Sal's taste, some restaurant desserts look like they came in a box; you can't always know. Instead: share one dessert, or ask which desserts are made in-house.
9. Skip 7: filling up on the free bread and salad before the main arrives. It's there to make the meal feel generous; enjoy some, but save room. Instead: ask for the bread with the meal.
10. What Sal does order: dishes that are hard or slow at home (braised meats, lasagna that takes hours, a well-made chicken parm with the sauce on top, not underneath), sharing plates, and the lunch menu for the same food at a lower price.
11. Payoff: Sal's 3-question rule before ordering — "Could I make this at home tonight in 20 minutes? Is it priced on the menu? Is it made here?" If yes, yes, no — think twice.
12. Outro: the Copycat Cookbook for the restaurant dishes worth making at home; tease the next video (the restaurant marinara secret).
