NARRATOR: Walter, a calm, experienced guide to checking your own house, speaking in first person to home buyers. Plain language, warm, practical. Never claims licenses, certifications or a specific career history.

STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments, mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: in a busy market, buyers hear "waive the inspection and your offer wins." It can feel like the only way to get the house. But once you close, whatever is wrong with the house is yours. Promise: 9 problems a home inspection commonly catches that a showing usually doesn't, and safer options than skipping it completely.
2. What an inspection is and isn't: a visual check of the house's main systems by a home inspector, based on a standards of practice (the InterNACHI standards are public). It isn't a guarantee and it doesn't look inside walls. Say this plainly so expectations are right.
3. Problem 1: the roof. From the driveway a roof can look fine. Inspectors commonly flag worn shingles, flashing problems and signs of past leaks in the attic.
4. Problem 2: water in the basement or crawlspace. Fresh paint, a dehumidifier running, new carpet only downstairs, or white powder on walls can point to past water. A showing in dry weather can hide it.
5. Problem 3: the electrical panel and wiring. Older panel brands commonly flagged by inspectors, DIY wiring, missing GFCI protection in kitchens and baths. Viewers should never open a panel themselves.
6. Problem 4: heating and cooling age and condition. A furnace or AC near the end of its life works fine on a 20-minute showing.
7. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description. It's a calm way to learn what to look at in any house.
8. Problem 5: the water heater. Age (from the label), rust at the base, signs of leaks, the relief valve and its discharge pipe.
9. Problem 6: plumbing leaks and drains. Slow drains, stains under sinks, low water pressure, older pipe types. Point out the separate sewer line camera inspection, which a standard inspection usually does not include.
10. Problem 7: structure and foundation. Which crack patterns are commonly flagged (stair-step, horizontal, wide or uneven), sloping floors, doors that stick. Serious concerns go to a structural engineer.
11. Problem 8: the attic. Ventilation, insulation, signs of moisture or pests, and old insulation types (vermiculite: don't disturb it).
12. Problem 9: safety items. Smoke and CO alarms, stair railings, garage door auto-reverse, gas appliance venting.
13. Safer options than skipping it (say clearly these depend on your contract, your state and your agent's advice; Walter isn't giving legal advice): an inspection "for information only" where you agree not to ask for small repairs; a quick walk-through with an inspector before you offer; targeted specialty checks like a sewer scope, radon test or chimney inspection; and going to the inspection yourself with questions.
14. Payoff: the 9 in one line each — roof, water, electrical, heating and cooling, water heater, plumbing and drains, structure, attic, safety items. A showing shows you the paint. An inspection shows you the systems.
15. Outro: for buyers, the Red Flag Checklist covers what to look for at a showing before you even make an offer (description). Tease the next video.
