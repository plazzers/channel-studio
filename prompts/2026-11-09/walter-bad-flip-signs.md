1. Hook: the photos look perfect. New floors, new kitchen, fresh paint everywhere. Many flips are done well. But some are only skin deep, and the paint can hide what you most need to see. Promise: 7 signs you can spot in about 10 minutes at a showing, and what to ask about each one.
2. Fair note: a flip isn't bad because it's a flip. The question is whether the work under the surface was done right, with permits where needed.
3. Sign 1: new surfaces, old systems. Look at the labels and age of the furnace, AC, water heater and the electrical panel. A brand-new kitchen next to a very old furnace tells you where the money went.
4. Sign 2: fresh paint in one spot. A single painted ceiling patch, a painted basement wall, or paint over stains. Ask why that spot was painted.
5. Sign 3: floors that don't sit right. Soft spots, bouncy areas, new flooring with gaps or slopes, a step-up between rooms that wasn't there before. New flooring can cover an uneven or damaged subfloor.
6. Sign 4: sloppy finish work. Gaps at trim, wavy caulk lines, crooked tiles, cabinet doors that don't close square, outlet covers that don't sit flat. If the parts you can see were rushed, ask about the parts you can't.
7. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description.
8. Sign 5: DIY electrical and plumbing. Outlets that wiggle, missing cover plates, no GFCI protection near sinks, a new light fixture with a dead switch, slow drains or drips under brand-new sinks. Look, test GFCI buttons, run water; never open panels or covers.
9. Sign 6: the bathroom tells the story. Soft floor near the toilet, missing or noisy exhaust fan, fresh caulk over old caulk, new tile around an old tub. Bathrooms hide water damage.
10. Sign 7: no paperwork. Ask for the list of work done, contractor names, receipts and permits for anything structural, electrical, plumbing, roofing or HVAC. Your agent can help check permit records with the local building department. Also look at the property's sale history; a fast resale isn't a red flag by itself, but it's a good reason to ask questions.
11. What to do: write down what you saw, share it with your home inspector, and consider extra checks like a sewer scope. Point to the free bad-flip guide and the questions-to-ask-the-seller guide.
12. Payoff: the 7 signs in one line each — new surfaces with old systems, paint in one spot, floors that don't sit right, sloppy finish work, DIY wiring and plumbing, the bathroom, no paperwork. Love the kitchen, but buy the house underneath it.
13. Outro: the Red Flag Checklist is made for showings like this one (description). Tease the next video.
