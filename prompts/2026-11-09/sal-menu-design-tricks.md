STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments, mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: a menu looks like a list of food. It isn't. It's one of the most carefully planned pieces of paper in the building. Promise: 9 design tricks menus commonly use, and how to read a menu so you order what you actually want.
2. Trick 1: the sweet spot. Menu consultants often put the dishes they most want to sell where eyes tend to land first, like the top of a section or the upper right of a two-page menu. Note honestly that research on exactly where eyes go is mixed; the habit is real.
3. Trick 2: the anchor. One very expensive dish at the top makes everything below it feel reasonable. Often it's there to be looked at, not ordered.
4. Trick 3: no dollar signs. "24" feels softer than "$24.00". Prices written as plain numbers or words.
5. Trick 4: no price column. Prices tucked at the end of the description instead of lined up on the right, so you read the food first and don't compare prices down the column.
6. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description.
7. Trick 5: boxes, bold and little icons. A box, a different color or a "chef's favorite" mark pulls the eye to the dishes the restaurant wants to move, often the higher-margin ones.
8. Trick 6: delicious words. "Slow-braised", "hand-rolled", "farm-fresh", "Nonna's". Descriptive words make a dish sound worth more. Sal's tip: ask what it actually means.
9. Trick 7: fewer choices per section. Short sections are easier to decide from and faster for the kitchen. A huge menu can be a reason to ask what's made in-house.
10. Trick 8: pictures or no pictures. Photos can sell a dish, and many sit-down places skip them on purpose to feel more upscale. Neither is a trick against you; just know the photo is the best-looking plate they ever made.
11. Trick 9: the separate menus. Drinks, desserts and specials on their own cards, offered at the moment you're most likely to say yes. The specials often come without a price said out loud; always ask.
12. How to read a menu like a chef (fast list): look at the whole menu before you decide, find the price range first, ask about the special's price, ask what's made in-house, decide on drinks and dessert before you sit down, share an appetizer.
13. Payoff: the 9 in one line each — sweet spot, anchor, no dollar signs, no price column, boxes and icons, delicious words, fewer choices, pictures, separate menus. None of it is a scam. It's design. And now you can see it.
14. Outro: the dishes people order most at the Italian and casual chains are easy at home — Sal's Restaurant Copycat Cookbook and Sal's Kitchen App (description). Tease the next video.
