1. Hook: most house trouble doesn't start with a disaster. It starts with a small job that got pushed to "next weekend" for a year. Promise: the 9 routine jobs that are easiest to skip and most worth doing, what each one takes, and which ones you can do yourself in an afternoon.
2. Job 1: the furnace or HVAC filter. Check it monthly in heating season, match the size, arrow toward the furnace, write the date on the frame. A clogged filter makes the system work harder.
3. Job 2: a yearly heating service. Have a qualified technician look at the furnace or boiler, especially fuel-burning ones. Mention that only a pro opens burners and gas parts. Tie in carbon monoxide alarms.
4. Job 3: the dryer vent. Clean the lint screen every load; have the vent duct cleaned out, and check the outside flap opens and isn't blocked by snow. NFPA lists failure to clean as a leading factor in dryer fires; say it plainly without numbers.
5. Job 4: gutters and downspouts. Clear them, make sure downspouts carry water several feet away from the foundation. Stay off ladders in ice and wind; hire help if a ladder isn't safe for you.
6. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description. It turns these jobs into one easy weekend.
7. Job 5: smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Press test on every one, check the date on the back (smoke alarms are replaced at 10 years per NFPA), and make sure there's one on every level.
8. Job 6: washing machine hoses. Look for bulges, cracks and rust at the ends; many people replace old rubber hoses with braided ones. Know where the two shutoff valves are.
9. Job 7: the sump pump, if you have one. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch it turn on and pump out. Check the discharge line outside isn't frozen or buried.
10. Job 8: caulk and gaps outside. Walk around the house and look at caulk around windows, doors and where pipes and wires go through walls. Fill small gaps to keep water, drafts and mice out.
11. Job 9: the water heater look-over. Look for rust or moisture at the base, note the age from the label, and keep the area around it clear. For flushing or testing the relief valve, follow the manual or have a plumber show you; never stand in front of the discharge pipe.
12. How to make it stick: put the jobs on a calendar, one or two a month, and do a 10-minute walk around the house once a month. Point to the maintenance-calendar and monthly-house-walk guides.
13. Payoff: the 9 in one line each — filter, heating service, dryer vent, gutters, alarms, washer hoses, sump pump, caulk, water heater. Pick two this weekend.
14. Outro: Walter's Home Check App keeps these jobs on a schedule with reminders (description). Tease the next video.
