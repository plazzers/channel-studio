NARRATOR: Walter, a calm, experienced guide to checking your own house, speaking in first person to homeowners. Plain language, warm, practical. Never claims licenses, certifications or a specific career history.

STRUCTURE: Hook under 1 minute that ends with a promise, numbered segments grouped in four parts (outside, at the walls, inside, habits), mid-video free PDF mention, payoff, outro with product mention and tease of the next video.
1. Hook: many old houses had basements long before pumps and membranes were common, and the dry ones usually had one thing in common: water was sent away from the house before it ever reached the walls. Promise: 20 simple, mostly free habits, and the clear signs that mean it's time to call a pro.
2. Honest note: most basement water starts outside, with roof water and the slope of the ground. These tricks help a lot with dampness and runoff; they don't fix structural cracks or a high water table.
3. Part 1, outside (tricks 1-7): 1) clean gutters; 2) downspouts carried several feet away, with extensions or splash blocks; 3) ground sloping away from the house; 4) keep soil and mulch below the siding and away from the walls; 5) don't let flower beds with edging trap water against the foundation; 6) clear window wells and use covers; 7) shovel snow away from the foundation and keep the storm drain at the curb clear.
4. Part 2, at the walls (tricks 8-12): 8) walk the outside wall after a heavy rain and watch where water goes; 9) seal gaps where pipes and wires enter; 10) watch patios, walks and driveways that tilt toward the house; 11) check that the sump discharge, if any, goes far from the house and isn't frozen; 12) keep shrubs trimmed back so air and sun reach the walls.
5. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description; it has a basement and drainage section.
6. Part 3, inside (tricks 13-17): 13) the tape test: tape a square of plastic to the wall for a couple of days to see if moisture comes through or condensation forms on the room side (keep it general, it's a clue, not a diagnosis); 14) run a dehumidifier in damp seasons and empty or drain it; 15) keep the bathroom and dryer venting outdoors, not into the basement; 16) store things on shelves and in plastic bins, off the floor and away from walls; 17) keep floor drains and their traps working.
7. Part 4, habits (tricks 18-20): 18) look in the basement after every big storm or thaw with a flashlight; 19) keep a simple log with dates and photos of any water; 20) know where the main water shutoff is, because not every wet basement is from outside, a leaking pipe or water heater can look the same.
8. When to call a pro: water coming in every rain, cracks that are growing, wide or with one side higher than the other, bowing walls, a sump that runs constantly, or mold growing on large areas. Mention the EPA's mold guidance in general terms: fix the moisture, and get help for big areas.
9. Safety: never stand in water near outlets, cords or appliances; turn off power to a wet basement only if you can reach the panel safely from a dry spot, otherwise call the utility or an electrician.
10. Payoff: the four parts in one line each — send water away outside, close up the walls, keep the inside dry, and look after storms. The old way was simple: don't let water get close.
11. Outro: The Home Check Manual has room-by-room checks, including basements and crawlspaces (description). Tease the next video.
