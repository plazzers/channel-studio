1. Hook: everybody has made sad garlic bread. Burnt edges, raw garlic bite, soggy middle, or dry like a cracker. The restaurant version is crisp outside, soft and buttery inside, garlicky but sweet. Promise: 5 mistakes to stop making and the restaurant way to fix each one, in about 15 minutes.
2. Mistake 1: the wrong bread. Too soft and it goes soggy; too thin and it dries out. A crusty Italian loaf or a split baguette works best; day-old bread is fine.
3. Mistake 2: melted butter poured on. Soften the butter instead and mix it into a paste so it stays on the surface and doesn't soak straight through. Olive oil can join it.
4. Mistake 3: raw or burnt garlic. Use fresh garlic, finely grated or minced, and either let it sit in the butter or warm it gently in a pan first so it goes mellow, not harsh. Burnt garlic turns bitter in seconds; that's why it goes on the bread, not straight under the broiler alone.
5. Mid-video: the free 25 Rules for Eating Out is the first link in the description.
6. Mistake 4: no seasoning. A pinch of salt, chopped parsley, and a little grated Parmesan or Pecorino. Optional pinch of red pepper flakes.
7. Mistake 5: one temperature, start to finish. The restaurant way: bake wrapped in foil first so the inside gets hot and soft, then open it and finish under the broiler for a minute or two, watching it the whole time.
8. Make-ahead for holidays: mix the garlic butter ahead and keep it in the fridge; spread the bread and wrap it, then bake when guests arrive. Store leftover garlic butter in the fridge and use it within a few days.
9. Point to Sal's free Garlic Butter Sauce recipe on the guide site (it's one of the free samples from the cookbook), and the breadsticks guide for the endless-basket style.
10. Payoff: the 5 fixes in one line each — the right loaf, soft butter not melted, mellow garlic, season it, foil then broiler. Better than the breadbasket.
