1. Hook: the first cold night, the space heater comes out of the closet, and most people set it up in a way the safety guides warn against. Promise: 7 mistakes, and a one-minute check you can do tonight.
2. Mistake 1: Things too close. The three-foot rule from NFPA: curtains, bedding, couch throws, clothes, paper, holiday decorations. Also a three-foot kid-and-pet-free zone.
3. Mistake 2: Plugging into an extension cord or power strip. NFPA says plug directly into a wall outlet. Feel the plug and outlet after a while: hot, discolored or a burning smell means turn it off and have an electrician look.
4. Mistake 3: Leaving it on when you leave the room or go to bed. "I'll be right back" is how heaters get forgotten.
5. Mistake 4: Setting it on a rug, bed, table or a stack of books. It belongs on a hard, level floor, out of walkways, with the cord not under a rug.
6. Mistake 5: Buying the cheapest one without safety features. Look for tip-over shut-off, overheat protection and a label from a recognized testing laboratory. Check old heaters' cords every season.
7. Mid-video: the free Before the First Freeze checklist is the first link in the description.
8. Mistake 6: Treating a fuel-burning heater like an electric one. Kerosene and propane heaters must be rated for indoor use, refueled outside when cool, and need working CO alarms. Never use grills, camp stoves, outdoor heaters or generators inside or in a garage. If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911 from outside.
9. Mistake 7: Using a space heater (or the oven) to make up for a heating system that can't keep up. If you need heaters in several rooms, have the furnace checked. Never heat the house with the oven.
10. Payoff: the one-minute check tonight — clear circle, wall outlet, off when you leave, CO alarm tested.
11. Outro: The Home Check Manual for the full room-by-room seasonal checks; tease next video on finding drafts in your house.
