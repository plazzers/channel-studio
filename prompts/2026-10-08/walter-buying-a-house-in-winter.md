1. Hook: winter can be a good time to buy, with fewer buyers and motivated sellers, but snow and cold hide things. Promise: 7 things to check or ask about so winter works for you, not against you.
2. The roof: snow covers shingles, so the inspector may not be able to walk or see it. Ask the roof's age and for paperwork, look for icicles and ice dams along the edges, and read what the inspection report says it couldn't see.
3. Grading and drainage: snow hides which way the ground slopes. Ask for photos from warmer months, look at where downspouts end, check the basement for stains, white powder (efflorescence) and a musty smell.
4. The AC: many inspectors won't run an air conditioner in cold weather. Ask its age and service records; plan for a check in spring if needed.
5. Mid-video: the free Weekend Home Check is the first link in the description.
6. The heat: winter is the best time to feel it. Walk every room. Is one much colder? Ask for 12 months of utility bills and the furnace's age and service history.
7. Drafts and windows: feel around windows and doors, look for fogging between double-pane glass, condensation and frost on the inside.
8. Pipes and plumbing: run every tap, look under sinks, ask if pipes have ever frozen, and check that the outside faucets and any crawlspace look protected. Vacant homes: ask whether the house was winterized and de-winterized properly before the inspection.
9. Driveway, walks and steps: snow and ice hide cracks and heaved concrete; ask for photos and walk them on a clear day if you can.
10. Payoff: a short winter buyer list — ask for photos, records and bills; read what the inspector couldn't see; plan follow-ups (roofer, HVAC tech, sewer scope) before closing.
11. Outro: The House Buyer's Red Flag Checklist for showings; tease next video.
